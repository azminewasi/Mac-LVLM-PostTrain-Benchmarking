Mac LLM/VLM Post-training Testing · visual question answering (SpatialEval spatial-map)

Post-training a small vision-language model on a MacBook: SFT vs RL on spatial maps

November 19, 2026 · Qwen3.5-0.8B and 2B with LoRA · Apple M5 MacBook Pro, 16 GB · 9 runs, 13 hours of training and evaluation

Code on GitHub · Blog version · GSM8K text post-training · math-story post-training · Inference benchmark

The question

The text-only studies showed what SFT and RL do to a small model on a MacBook. Does the same recipe work when the model has to look at an image, and what does it cost the machine?

We used MilaWang/SpatialEval, spatial-map split: each item is a picture of a map with named locations and a multiple-choice question about it, in three types: direction ("in which direction is X relative to Y?"), which object ("which object is in the northwest of Y?") and count ("how many objects are to the east of Y?"). We trained on a small slice, 500 items, and tested on 100 held-out items, because the point is to benchmark what the Mac does, not to chase a leaderboard. The same six recipes as before ran on Qwen3.5-0.8B; three of them were repeated on Qwen3.5-2B. All use LoRA (rank 16) on the language model, with the vision tower frozen.

The short answers

  • Both families of recipe work, and they work fast. On 0.8B, the untrained model scores 39%; after training, SFT reaches 89%, gentle SFT 91%, and the RL methods GRPO 84%, RLOO 88%, GSPO 87%. Scores come from 100 questions and one seed, so differences of up to about ten points are noise.
  • Most of the failure was format and length, not vision. The untrained model wrote 392 tokens per answer on average, 29% of its answers hit the 640-token cap, and 24% never produced the answer line. Training mostly taught it to finish and to commit.
  • Training changes inference cost by an order of magnitude. The same questions cost 1006 generated tokens per correct answer untrained, 9 after SFT and 44 after GSPO; one answer takes 5.6 s untrained and 0.25 s after SFT on the MacBook.
  • The 2B model fits for RL on 16 GB, barely. GRPO peaked at 7.7 GB of MLX memory with up to 6.5 GB of swap, and the first RL step took 43 s against about 32 s for the first five steps of 0.8B. It was not better than 0.8B after RL: 85% vs 84%.
  • The skill transfers a little, and not predictably. On a different task (maze navigation) trained models scored 55%-67% against 25% chance, but the ranking of recipes changes, and one recipe (self-distillation) collapsed.

1. Setup

Images go through a frozen vision tower once; everything we train is the language model.

  • Model path. Qwen3.5 has a hybrid language model (gated delta-net and attention layers) and a vision tower. Maps are resized to 448 px, which becomes exactly 196 image tokens in the prompt (the mean prompt, image plus question and chat template, is 304 tokens). Vision features are computed once per image and cached; LoRA (rank 16, scale 20) sits on the language-model layers only, about 1.25% of the 0.8B parameters.
  • Data. 500 training items and 100 test items, seeded, drawn from the spatial-map shard; question types are mixed as in the source (Direction: 31, Which object: 30, Count: 39 in the test set).
  • Six recipes. SFT (answer-only targets, The answer is: C. Northwest, lr 2e-5, 5 epochs), gentle SFT (lr 5e-6, 2 epochs), self-distilled SFT (rejection-sample the model's own correct answers, then train on them), and three critic-free RL methods: GRPO, RLOO and GSPO (4 prompts × 4 sampled answers per step, temperature 1.0, correctness-only reward that also needs the answer line, no KL penalty, lr 2e-6, 5 epochs = 625 steps).
  • Scoring. An answer counts only if it finishes within 640 new tokens and ends in a parsable The answer is: X line with the right letter. This is strict on purpose: it is also the RL reward.

Direction and which-object questions are only partly answered correctly (61% and 57%); count questions are almost never answered correctly (8%), and 41% of them ran into the token cap while the model enumerated objects one by one. Chance is 25% on four options, and the most common gold letter covers 28% of the test set, so a model that only guessed the most common letter would score about 28%.

3. Supervised fine-tuning

Five epochs of answer-only SFT lifts the 0.8B model from 39% to 89%.

The SFT targets contain only the final line, taken from the dataset's oracle option. That is a deliberate simplification: there are no reference reasoning traces for these maps, so SFT teaches the model to answer directly. It works because the model already has the visual skill; it only needed to commit.

The aggressive recipe (lr 2e-5, 5 epochs) climbs slowly (39%, 47%, 64%, 76%, 86%, 89%); the gentle recipe (lr 5e-6, 2 epochs) gets to 91% in well under half the time. Self-distilled SFT ends at only 63%: it trains on the model's own correct answers, so it can only reinforce what the model already did, including the rambling style, and its training set was thin on the hard type: only 19% of its examples were count questions (88 of 457), against 39% of the test set, because the untrained model rarely got those right.

What the examples show

  • The untrained model reasons correctly and then answers wrongly. Above, it correctly says Jackal's Jams is "up and to the left", then maps that to Southwest and ticks option A. Its visual reading was right; the translation to a letter was not. This is why format-and-commit training pays off so quickly.
  • SFT removes the reasoning altogether. Seven tokens: The answer is: C. Northwest. It is the cheapest and, here, among the most accurate recipes, because the model has to read the map and answer in one step.
  • Self-distillation keeps the old style. It still writes a long worked answer, and it still makes the same kind of mistake on counts.
  • The RL methods each found a different style, from GRPO's long paragraph, through RLOO's short bullet list, to GSPO's near-direct answer (158, 87 and 38 tokens on average). None was rewarded for style; they drifted to whatever earned reward at the lowest cost.

Training changed how many tokens each answer costs, and with them the time per answer.

The untrained model writes 392 tokens per answer, 1006 per correct answer. SFT writes 8; GSPO 38; RLOO 87; GRPO 158. Dividing generated tokens by correct answers is the number that matters for cost: 109x fewer for SFT and 23x fewer for GSPO than the untrained model.

Accuracy is only half of a benchmark. This chapter reads the system counters logged at every step to see what the machine did while it trained: where the time went, how busy the GPU and CPU were, and whether the laptop ever slowed down.

Across 9 runs (13 hours of training in total) the GPU was busy 69% of the time on average and the CPU only 14%: training is a GPU job, and the CPU mostly feeds it. The thermal speed limit never dropped below 100%, so the laptop never throttled, even through multi-hour runs on a plugged-in laptop.

An RL step is mostly generation

Each RL step first samples 16 answers and then trains on them. Sampling took 68-81% of the step time (74% on average). Sampling is memory-bandwidth bound exactly as in the inference benchmark, so the lever that matters is how many tokens you generate, not how clever the optimiser is. Batching helps a lot: 16 sequences decoded together reach 175-377 tokens/s in total, against about 64 tokens/s for a single stream on the same model.

Where it ran out of room

Swap in use during GRPO: the 2B run pushed several GB to disk.
0.8B GRPO2B GRPO

  • Memory is the limit, not compute. The 2B GRPO run needed micro-batch 1 and peaked at 7.7 GB of MLX memory with system RAM at 14.0 GB and up to 6.5 GB of swap. It finished, but it left little headroom: longer answers, more images per prompt or a bigger model would push hard on 16 GB. We did not try to train 4B or larger models, so where exactly the limit lies is untested.
  • Images cost sequence length. Each prompt carries 196 image tokens (and about 300 tokens in total), so the sequence limit was 1,100 tokens and the images were downscaled to 448 px; the number of images per example was kept at one.
  • No thermal throttling. As in the text studies, the laptop never slowed itself, even through multi-hour RL runs.
  • Evaluation is not free. One 100-question evaluation of the untrained 2B model took about 160 s, as long as several RL steps, because untrained answers are long. RL training also spent most of each step in generation, so cost scales with answer length, not with the optimiser.
  • Everything was measured with one machine and one seed. The timing numbers should be read as typical for this laptop under this setup, not as hardware limits.

10. Conclusions

  1. On a small visual QA task, a few hundred examples and a few hours were enough to take a 0.8B model from 39% to roughly 85-90%, with either SFT or RL.
  2. Most of the gain is format and commitment: finishing within the token budget and producing the answer line. Reading the image was mostly already possible.
  3. RL needs no reference answers and learns its own short style; SFT is cheaper (minutes) when targets are as simple as a letter.
  4. Self-distillation did not work here: it cannot teach what the model never gets right.
  5. Training reduced cost per correct answer by roughly 109x with the cheapest recipe, because per-token speed is constant and answer length is what changed.
  6. The 2B model fits for RL on a 16 GB MacBook, but only just, and it did not beat 0.8B on this task.

Caveats

One seed per recipe and 100 test questions (±10 points at 95%). A single dataset slice of a templated, synthetic task. The maze-navigation results have no control (blank image, larger token cap), so transfer is suggestive only. Single-stream speed was measured on 0.8B only; the 2B figures are training and batched-evaluation numbers. We froze the vision tower; training it was not attempted.

B. Method and data notes

  • Data: SpatialEval spatial-map, vision-QA split, shard 0; 500 train / 100 test, seed 0; images resized to 448 px (longest side). Maze test: SpatialEval maze-navigation, 100 random questions, seed 0.
  • Hyperparameters: LoRA rank 16, scale 20 (language model only); SFT lr 2e-5 (5 epochs); gentle and self-distilled SFT lr 5e-6 (2 epochs); RL lr 2e-6, 4 prompts × 4 rollouts per step, temperature 1.0, up to 640 new tokens, no KL, 5 epochs; evaluation greedy, 100 questions, 25 at a time.
  • Software: MLX 0.32 and mlx-vlm 0.7.12 on macOS 26, Apple M5, 16 GB; the vision features are cached per image and the language model runs in training mode so the differentiable gated-delta kernel is used.
  • W&B project: macbook-posttrain-spatial. Metrics, rollouts and evaluation answers are kept under runs_spatial/; adapter weights were not committed.
