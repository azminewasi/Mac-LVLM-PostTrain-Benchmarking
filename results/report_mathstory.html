Teaching a 0.8B model to ignore distractors: SFT vs RL on math story problems

November 18, 2026 · Qwen3.5-0.8B with LoRA · Apple M5 MacBook Pro, 16 GB · 6 runs (still running: GSPO)

The question

On GSM8K, the model's own reasoning was already strong, and supervised fine-tuning made it worse. Would the picture change on a dataset where the answers are short and the failure mode is different?

We used azminetoushikwasi/math-story-problems. Every item is one arithmetic problem, three numbers joined by two plus or minus signs, written four ways: a bare equation, a short story, a story with distractor item types, and a long story with many irrelevant sentences. The answer is always a single integer. That makes it a clean test of one skill: pull the right numbers out of a story and calculate.

We trained on 500 items (each shown in one randomly chosen story form) and tested on 100 held-out items in all four forms, so each score rests on 400 prompts. Six recipes were compared on the same model: three kinds of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and three reinforcement-learning (RL) methods.

The short answers

  • The untrained model fails on length, not arithmetic. It gets 90% of bare equations right but only 8% of long stories, and 18% of its answers overall run out of tokens.
  • SFT works here, when the reference solutions are executable. Equation-derived solutions took the model from 56% to 94%, and a gentler learning rate to 97.8%. Training on the model's own correct answers barely helped (64%).
  • RL needs no reference solutions and does as well or better. GRPO reached 96% after one epoch, RLOO reached 99.5% after one epoch; final scores: GRPO 99%, RLOO 98% (GSPO still running).
  • RL also makes the model concise. Mean answer length fell from 302 tokens to 149 (GRPO), 73 (RLOO).

1. Where the untrained model fails

It can do the arithmetic. It cannot cope with long stories.

Untrained accuracy and truncation by question form (100 test questions each).
accuracyanswers cut off at the 512-token cap

Accuracy falls steadily as the story gets longer: 90% for the bare equation, 74% for the short story, 54% with distractor items, and 8% for the long story. In the long-story form the model writes about 484 tokens on average, and 65% of its answers are cut off at the 512-token cap, so it is failing as much by rambling as by getting things wrong.

2. Supervised fine-tuning: it works, if the targets are executable

Imitating short, correct arithmetic lifts the model from 56% to as high as 97.8%.

The dataset has only final answers, so for SFT we generated the reference solutions from the equation: 2385 + 761 = 3146, then 3146 - 1063 = 2083, then The answer is: 2083. Three variants were tried: the original recipe (learning rate 2e-5, 5 epochs), a gentle one (5e-6, 2 epochs), and self-distillation, which trains on the model's own correct finished answers.

The equation-derived solutions are a very different story from GSM8K's human-written ones. The model learns a ~42-token style it can carry out reliably, and the long-story form jumps from 8% to 96%. The gentle recipe gets there in a single epoch and never shows the early dip on bare equations that the original recipe does. Self-distillation, by contrast, only reinforces what the model already does: its data covers only the 65% of questions the model already solves, so it adds little where the model is weakest (the long story reaches 20%).

Takeaway. SFT quality depends on whether the reference solutions are something the model can execute. Short, correct, templated solutions work; on GSM8K the human solutions did not.

3. Reinforcement learning: the same reward, no reference solutions

The RL methods reach the top of the dataset, and they get there fast.

GRPO, RLOO and GSPO see only the questions and a correct-or-not reward: no reference solutions, no hints about the equation. Learning rate 2e-6, four sampled answers per question, up to 640 new tokens.

Training reward (share of sampled answers that finish and are correct), 25-step moving average.

GRPO scored 96% on the test set after a single epoch and RLOO scored 99.5% on the test set after a single epoch. By the end GRPO reached 99%, RLOO reached 98% while GSPO is still training. At this level (about 400 prompts, one seed) the differences between methods are within noise; what is clear is that RL matches the best SFT recipe without needing its reference solutions.

RL also changes how the model writes

Because there is no length penalty, the answer length shrinking is a by-product: shorter answers finish more often and so earn more reward.

Why the late steps change little

Once the model answers almost everything correctly, most question groups have four identical rewards and therefore no gradient: by the end, 98% (GRPO), 98.8% (RLOO) of groups carry no signal. Training keeps running, but very little changes after the first epochs.

On GSM8K the model already reasons well in its own long, step-by-step style, supervised fine-tuning on human solutions pulled it away from that style, and RL gave gains of 5 to 13 points (up to 16 with full fine-tuning). Here the untrained model is held back by rambling, a short templated style is something it can execute, and so both SFT on clean solutions and RL give very large gains. The shared lesson: post-training helps when it teaches something the model can actually do, and the cheapest reliable signal is a verifiable reward.

6. Conclusions

  1. On a clean, verifiable task, RL with a correctness-only reward matched the best supervised recipe without reference solutions.
  2. SFT works when reference solutions are short, correct and executable; a gentler learning rate was better than the aggressive one.
  3. Self-distillation preserves a model's style but cannot teach what the model never gets right.
  4. RL makes answers shorter as a side effect, which also made the RL runs several times cheaper than their GSM8K counterparts.
  5. After the first epoch, most RL groups carry no signal, which suggests shorter training would have been enough (not tested).

Caveats

One seed per recipe and 400 test prompts (100 per form), so differences of a few points are not meaningful near the 98-100% ceiling. The SFT targets are an easy deterministic template. Test equations never appear in the training data, but the problem templates do, so this measures skill on this family of problems, not general math. Late in some RL runs (e.g. RLOO), accuracy slipped a few points as answers kept shrinking; the final-epoch score is reported.

B. Method and data notes

  • Data: train 500 items from the 131,072-item training split (seeded; each uses one of the three story forms); evaluation on the first 100 items of the 3,072-item test split, in all four forms; greedy decoding, 512-token cap.
  • Hyperparameters: LoRA rank 16, scale 20; SFT lr 2e-5 (5 epochs); gentle SFT and self-distilled SFT lr 5e-6 (2 epochs); RL lr 2e-6, 4 prompts x 4 rollouts per step, temperature 1.0, up to 640 new tokens, correctness-only reward, 5 epochs; gradient checkpointing, clipping 1.0.
  • W&B project: macbook-posttrain-mathstory; all rollouts, evaluation answers and adapters are kept under runs_mathstory/. See also the research journal (JOURNAL.md) and the GSM8K report.
